# Retrieval 14: Booting Linux to a shell

## R1
Q: A Linux boot log ends with `timer_probe: no matching timers found`, then `Kernel panic - not syncing: Unable to initialise architected timer.`, and `macvmm` exits 5 after `--timeout`. What is wrong with the machine, and why did the VMM not stop on its own?
A: The device tree has no `/timer` node (or one whose `compatible` Linux does not match), so stage 4 of the boot cannot find a clock and panics. A panicked kernel spins inside the guest without touching anything that traps, so the run loop sees no exit; only `--timeout` (exit 5) ends the run.

## R2
Q: A boot log says `GICv3: No redistributor present`, then shows an Oops and a panic, while the VMM prints no unhandled-MMIO error. Which two numbers do you compare to find the bug?
A: The redistributor base in the device tree's `/intc` node (its second `reg` pair) and the base where the interrupt controller really has its redistributors (`GICR_BASE`, `0x080a_0000` in our layout). Linux read `GICR_PIDR2` at offset `0xFFE8` of the frame it was told about; our read-as-zero region answered 0, which is not a GICv3 revision, so nothing looked broken to the VMM.

## R3
Q: A boot exits 0 and reaches `reboot: Power down`, but the log has no `ttyAMA0 at MMIO` line, says `Warning: unable to open an initial console.`, and the initramfs's `ready` line is missing. What did Linux fail to do, and which part of our machine do you check first?
A: It never bound a driver to the PL011 node, so no `ttyAMA0` console exists and user-space output has nowhere to go (kernel messages still appear through `earlycon`). Check what the AMBA bus matches on: the eight identification registers at `0xFE0` to `0xFFC` (expect `0x11 0x10 0x14 0x00 0x0d 0xf0 0x05 0xb1`), then the node's `compatible` and its `apb_pclk` clock.

## R4
Q: A guest has panicked and `macvmm` is still running. Without stopping it, how do you see what the vCPU last did, and how do you tell "spinning without exits" from "busy doing I/O"?
A: Press `Ctrl-A` then `t` in its terminal, or send it SIGUSR1 from another (`kill -USR1 <pid>`). The last 256 exits of each vCPU are printed on standard error, oldest first. Compare the newest record's `t_ns` with how long the VM has been running: a last exit seconds ago means the vCPU is looping on something we do not trap.

## R5
Q: A Linux boot wrote 12,093 bytes to the PL011 and caused 32,811 data-abort exits. About how many exits per byte is that, and why more than one?
A: About 2.7. Each byte is at least two device accesses, each its own VM exit: a read of the flag register `UARTFR` to check there is room, the store to `UARTDR`, and often a further read of `UARTFR`.

## R6
Q: Why does adding `panic=-1` to the kernel command line turn a panicking kernel's run from exit status 5 into exit status 4 (with `--on-reset exit`)?
A: `panic=-1` makes Linux reboot immediately after a panic instead of spinning. On our machine a reboot is a PSCI `SYSTEM_RESET` hypercall, which the VMM sees as an exit, and `--on-reset exit` maps it to status 4. Without it the kernel spins forever and only the timeout (5) ends the run.

## R7
Q: You want a trace of a Linux boot that holds only the guest's hypercalls and trapped system registers, not the 32,000 console accesses. What do you pass, and roughly how many lines do you get?
A: `--trace exits:FILE:hvc,sysreg`. The categories after the second colon select records by exception class (`0x16` for `hvc`, `0x18` for system registers); our boot gives 18 lines (16 PSCI and SMCCC calls, 2 debug-register writes) instead of 32,829.

## R8
Q: Linux reads `0x11 0x10 0x14 0x00` from the four peripheral-ID registers of a PrimeCell. What part number, designer and revision is that?
A: The low byte of each register, assembled little-end first, gives `0x00141011`: bits 11 to 0 are the part number `0x011` (a PL011), bits 19 to 12 the designer `0x41` (Arm), bits 23 to 20 the revision 1. Linux printed it as "PL011 rev1".
