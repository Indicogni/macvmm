# Retrieval 12: Why an interrupt controller, and why in the kernel

## R1
Q: A device tree node says `interrupts = <0 5 4>` under a GICv3. Which INTID will the device's interrupt arrive as, and how is it triggered?
A: INTID 37. The first cell 0 means SPI (shared peripheral interrupt), and SPI *n* is INTID 32 + *n*, so SPI 5 is INTID 37. The third cell 4 means level-sensitive, active high.

## R2
Q: The `/timer` node lists `<1 11 4>` for the virtual timer. Which INTID is that, and why is it not an SPI?
A: INTID 27. The first cell 1 means PPI (private peripheral interrupt), and PPI *n* is INTID 16 + *n*. Each CPU has its own timer, so its interrupt is private to that CPU and lives in that CPU's redistributor, not in the shared distributor.

## R3
Q: An interrupt handler reads `ICC_IAR1_EL1` and gets 1023. What happened, and should it write `ICC_EOIR1_EL1`?
A: 1023 is the spurious INTID: nothing was pending by the time the CPU asked (another CPU took it, or the line dropped). There is nothing to handle and nothing was made active, so the handler returns without an end-of-interrupt write.

## R4
Q: Your VMM calls `hv_gic_create` right after creating vCPU 0. What comes back, and where must the call go instead?
A: An error, `HV_BUSY` on our Mac (before `hv_vm_create` it is `HV_NO_DEVICE`). The GIC must be created after the VM and before any vCPU, because each vCPU gets its own redistributor and CPU-interface state when it is created.

## R5
Q: A UART model raises its level line when a byte arrives but never lowers it. The guest's handler reads the byte and writes EOI. What does the guest see next?
A: The same interrupt again, at once, forever: at EOI the GIC sees the line still high and makes it pending again. The guest makes no progress (an interrupt storm). The device must lower the line when the cause is gone: the FIFO drained, the cause cleared in `ICR`, or the source masked in `IMSC`.

## R6
Q: `GicSpi::new` is asked to wire INTID 27 to a device. What does it answer, and why is refusing at machine build time better than letting `hv_gic_set_spi` fail later?
A: It refuses with `NotAnSpi`: 27 is a PPI (the virtual timer), outside the SPI range 32..1020. Refusing at build time turns a wiring bug into a start-up error with a message; a later failure would happen on a path the guest triggers (every UART access) and could only be logged and ignored.

## R7
Q: A 2-vCPU machine puts its redistributors at `0x080A_0000`. How much space must the GIC node's second `reg` entry cover, and where does vCPU 1's redistributor start?
A: Two frames of 128 KiB (`0x2_0000`), so `0x4_0000` bytes. The second frame starts at `0x080C_0000`; the frames follow the order in which the vCPUs were created, so the guest finds its own by reading each frame's affinity.

## R8
Q: The chapter 11 timer guest made 10 VTIMER and 10 WFI exits for ten 100 ms sleeps. How many of each does the same workload make with the in-kernel GIC, and why?
A: Zero of each. The kernel delivers the virtual timer's interrupt through its own GIC and handles WFI by parking the vCPU until an interrupt is pending, so neither event needs our VMM. What remains are the exits our devices cause, such as UART register accesses.

## R9
Q: A key arrives while the guest's only vCPU sleeps in WFI under the in-kernel GIC. Which thread raises the interrupt, and what does it call?
A: The stdin reader thread: it pushes the byte into the UART model, which recomputes its level and calls `hv_gic_set_spi(33, true)` through its `IrqLine`. That call works from any thread and wakes the vCPU inside the kernel; no kick and no VM exit is needed.
